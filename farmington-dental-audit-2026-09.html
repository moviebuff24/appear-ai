ChatGPT Doesn't Name the Practice

Across its four answers, ChatGPT recommended 19 different practices. Farmington Dental Studio was not one of them, and ChatGPT did not cite farmingtondentalstudio.com as a source even once.

In this audit, ChatGPT's answers leaned on review directories rather than practice websites. Its two most-cited sources were Birdeye (13 citations) and dentistrydirectories.com (9 citations). Here is how the practice appears on each.

Both of the directories ChatGPT leans on carry the practice under the same longer name, and neither matches the Google profile. dentistrydirectories.com adds a phone number that doesn't match the website and a second listing for Dr. Eiwaz under a third phone number, so instead of one listing with 391 reviews it shows two smaller, out-of-date ones. Its review count trails Google's by 137. Birdeye's trails by 90. Both are working from an older copy of the data, and nothing will update either one unless someone corrects it.

The Birdeye profile exists, but it is unclaimed and on page two Birdeye's Farmington Hills dental directory runs to ten pages. Page one is where ChatGPT's picks come from: Midwestern Dental, Desai Dental Spa, Center for Implant Dentistry and Oral Surgery, and Richard S. Bernstein DDS were all recommended by ChatGPT. Farmington Dental Studio sits on page two. Seven of the ten practices on page one have fewer Birdeye reviews than it does, so review volume is not what is holding it back. The profile itself is live and still collecting reviews, the most recent posted six days ago, and it carries the correct address and website link. But Birdeye shows it as never claimed by the practice, and it is filed under the longer name rather than the one on the Google profile.
03

Implants: A Specialist the Platforms Can't See

Implants are the highest-value treatment on the menu, and the practice has a board-certified periodontist on the team. Asked for the best implant dentist in Farmington Hills, three of four platforms didn't mention the practice at all.

The credential is real and it is on the website. The About page presents Dr. Azimi as one of the two doctors and credits him as a Board-Certified Periodontist from the American Board of Periodontology. That is precisely the qualification an implant question is looking for.

The problem is how quietly it is said. He is labelled "Dr. Khash, DDS, MS", leading with a nickname rather than his full name and his specialty, so the word "periodontist" never appears where a machine reading the page would attach it to him. At least two other metro Detroit dental websites describe him as a "Board Certified Periodontist & Dental Implant Specialist." Perplexity read the practice's own /implant-dentist/ page while answering the implant question and still didn't name the practice.

Three Phone Numbers, One Suite

AI platforms confirm who a business is by matching its name, address and phone number across the web. Every mismatch weakens that match. Three different numbers currently point to Suite 140.

Some of these may be call-tracking numbers that all ring the same front desk, which works fine for patients. For AI platforms, each one is a separate claim about who the business is. Today, two AI answers to the same question gave patients two different numbers for the practice.

✗
Healthgrades has the wrong address and no reviews

The practice listing shows 32910 W 13 Mile Rd, Ste B200, an old address on a different road, with the 516-0004 number and zero reviews. Dr. Eiwaz's profile also has zero ratings. Claude and Perplexity cited Healthgrades 20 times between them in this audit.

✗
The website's structured data has a broken link to the Google profile

The site's Dentist markup is supposed to point AI platforms to the Google Business Profile. That entry is two URLs joined into one string, so it doesn't resolve. The same markup also doesn't include office hours.

05

What's Already Working

The foundations are better than the scorecard suggests, which is why the fixes above are listing work rather than a rebuild.

✓
Two of the AI platforms read the website in almost every answer.

Claude and Perplexity cited farmingtondentalstudio.com in seven of their eight answers. The site is crawlable, AI crawlers aren't blocked, and structured data is in place. The content is getting through. What's missing is agreement between the site and everything else online.

✓
Three #1 recommendations, and a strong Google profile behind them.

Google AI Mode made the practice its "Top Pick for Overall Care & Flexibility" for new patients, and describes it as "a highly-rated general and cosmetic dental practice." Perplexity put it first on both cosmetic dentistry and new patients. The Google profile carries 4.9 stars across 391 reviews, comparable to the implant specialists named in this audit.

The Perplexity wins are more fragile than they look Perplexity's stated reason for the #1 cosmetic ranking was the site calling itself the "#1 Cosmetic Dental Office." Claude explicitly discounts that kind of claim: "every practice calls itself the best — Farmington Dental Studio, for instance, bills itself as 'the #1 dental office for dental implants' — so marketing language shouldn't drive your choice." Rankings built on the practice describing itself can disappear the next time Perplexity changes how it weighs sources. Rankings backed by consistent third-party listings hold up.

Ordered by expected impact. None of them involve rebuilding the website.

09

What's Next

The platforms already know the practice is good. They can't tell which listing is the practice.

Where AI platforms work from the website and the Google profile, Farmington Dental Studio does well: three #1 recommendations, and a website Claude and Perplexity cited in seven of eight answers. Where they work from directories, they find a longer name, three phone numbers, a separate doctor listing, an old address, and an unclaimed profile on page two. That is the most likely reason ChatGPT never names the practice, and why the implant question goes to other practices.

None of this requires a new website. It's a quarter of careful, unglamorous listing work, and the answers to the three questions above decide where it starts.
