01 · The question

Where does the search
actually happen now?

For twenty years the answer was simple. Someone typed "dentist near me," scanned a page of links, and picked from what they saw. Your marketing budget was built for that page.

That page still exists. It is just no longer the only place the decision gets made, and increasingly it is not even the first one.

03 · The number that matters

Consumers who use ChatGPT or similar AI tools to get a local business recommendation

45% UP FROM 6% ONE YEAR EARLIER

That is a sevenfold jump in twelve months. It moves AI into third place among all sources people use to find a local business, behind Google and Facebook and ahead of everything else.

  • Trust is already there. More consumers say they trust AI results for local searches (40%) than say they do not (32%).
  • It is treated like a review. 42% trust an AI recommendation as much as they trust traditional reviews.

BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026. Representative panel of 1,002 US adult consumers via SurveyMonkey. Figures are across all local business categories; the survey does not publish a dental-specific cut.

04 · The honest version

Google is not dead. The front door changed shape.

  • Google still holds roughly 90% of search traffic. This is not a pitch to abandon it, and anyone telling you to is selling something.
  • But Google itself became an answer engine. 2.5 billion people a month now meet it through an AI Overview, and AI answers appear on close to half of all queries.
  • So the question was never ChatGPT versus Google. Both hand your patient a generated answer. The only thing that varies is whether your practice is in it.
Why waiting is the expensive choice

Six percent to 45% in twelve months. Wherever the ceiling turns out to be, the direction is not ambiguous, and the practices being named in these answers today are simply the ones that got measured first.

These answers lean on a small set of sources. A practice with clean, consistent, well-structured information gets picked up out of proportion to its size right now, because almost nobody in dentistry has given the models anything to work with. That advantage narrows as the field fills in.

Statcounter via industry reporting, Mar 2026 · Google I/O, Jun 2026 · BrightLocal 2026. One caution worth keeping in mind: chatbot usage, AI referral traffic, and share of searches showing an AI answer are three different measurements, and they get quoted interchangeably in the trade press.

06 · The part that surprises people

Ranking well on Google does not mean the AI recommends you.

These are two different systems making two different selections. A practice can sit in the Google map pack and be entirely absent from the answer a patient actually reads.

  • Roughly 45% overlap between businesses that rank well in traditional local search and businesses that appear in AI recommendations. Fewer than half carry over.
  • 65% of AI Overview citations do not rank in Google's top 10 for the same query. The AI is citing sources the blue links never showed you.
  • AI recommendation behaves as binary, not incremental. On Google you climb from eleventh to seventh to fourth. In an AI answer you are named or you are not.
  • The quality bar sits higher. ChatGPT has been observed recommending practices averaging about 4.3 stars and up. Google Maps still surfaces businesses in the 3.5 to 4.0 range.

These four are vendor and agency studies (Local Falcon, Whitespark and others), not peer-reviewed work. Treat them as directional. They are consistent with each other and with what we see running audits, which is why they are here.

08 · The gap

Patients moved. Practices have not.

45%
of consumers now ask an AI for a local recommendation.
?
How many practices are adapting to the shift?
This is the whole argument

Every practice already knows its Google ranking. Almost none know whether they appear when a patient asks ChatGPT for an orthodontist in their city, what name the model uses for them, which sources it is reading, or which competitor it names instead. That is a measurable thing, and almost nobody is measuring it.

09 · How we measure it

We ask the machines what your patients ask them.

A set of real questions, run against every major assistant, scored the same way each time. Your set is built around the procedures you actually want to be found for.

The questions

  • Best dentist in your city
  • Dentist accepting new patients near me
  • Who does Invisalign in your city
  • Emergency dentist, open now
  • Best pediatric dentist nearby
  • Plus the procedures that carry your margin

What we capture on every answer

  • Whether you appear at all. The first and bluntest question.
  • Where you land. Named first, or eighth in a list nobody finishes reading.
  • What it read to decide. The specific sources behind the recommendation.
  • What name it used for you. Entity confusion is common and quietly costly.
  • The reason it gave. Models explain their picks, which tells you what to fix.
  • Who it named instead. Your real competitive set here, often not the one you assume.
